**WoW Forever is set in the first year of World of Warcraft, a few years after the Third War and after the Warcraft III: Reforged Forsaken Kingdom campaign, and before Molten Core.** It follows its own timeline: it is not Classic and it does not replay later expansions. The world is recovering from war, old kingdoms have fallen, alliances are fragile and unexplored places are starting to matter again.

You do not need twenty years of Warcraft knowledge to enjoy it. Four ideas cover the essentials: Azeroth has deep ancient history, the Alliance and Horde are coalitions with their own tensions, the fall of Lordaeron reshaped the human and undead worlds, and Forever extends these stories along new paths.

## WoW Forever's timeline in five steps

- **Ancient Azeroth:** elves, trolls, Titans, elemental forces and old ruins create the world's deep history.
- **Wars of humans and orcs:** repeated conflicts shape the kingdoms and their alliances.
- **Warcraft III:** the Scourge, the Burning Legion and the fall of Lordaeron transform the world.
- **Forsaken Kingdom campaign:** a newer Warcraft III: Reforged campaign explores the Forsaken and the founding of the Undercity.
- **WoW Forever:** original Azeroth grows along its own timeline, with new regions, quests and mysteries.

This is an orientation, not a full chronology. Later events from modern WoW should not be treated as facts in Forever's timeline.

## What kind of world is Azeroth?

Azeroth is a world of many peoples, climates, ruins and magical traditions. Its places are more than level brackets: geography reflects earlier migrations, wars and alliances, and old structures keep traces of civilizations that no longer exist as they were.

The **Eastern Kingdoms** hold human kingdoms, dwarven strongholds and the aftermath of Lordaeron's fall. **Kalimdor** holds wilderness, Night Elf history, the orcish homeland around Durotar and cultures shaped by nature and the elements. Forever returns to these two continents and explores the mysteries and overlooked spaces within them.

## What happened during Warcraft III?

In broad terms, the Scourge, the Burning Legion, humans, orcs, Night Elves and many other peoples fought devastating wars. Old assumptions about safe kingdoms and lasting alliances collapsed.

The largest consequence was the **fall of Lordaeron**, once the most powerful human kingdom. On Kalimdor, rival groups had to face a threat greater than their quarrels, and the defeat of **Archimonde** at Mount Hyjal ended the invasion. That matters for Forever because Blizzard has announced new storytelling on Mount Hyjal, where the new raids will open on December 9.

Remember one thing: the world has recent trauma, and its peoples disagree about how to rebuild.

## Why Lordaeron and the Forsaken matter

Lordaeron's fall changed the balance between the living and the dead. The **Forsaken** are undead people who broke free of the Scourge and now seek their own path, ruled from the Undercity by Lady Sylvanas Windrunner. They are not simply monsters in a ruined city.

The Forsaken Kingdom campaign explores the founding of the Undercity and the origins of the first Forsaken Paladin. It is useful background for why Forever adds Undead (Forsaken) Paladins and the **Ruins of Lordaeron** dungeon. Someone who enters those ruins without context sees broken walls and enemies. Someone who knows the story sees a struggle over memory and the chance to reclaim a home.

## The Alliance and the Horde

The **Alliance** is a coalition of Humans, Dwarves, Gnomes and Night Elves. In Forever's time, Stormwind is ruled by the boy-king Anduin Wrynn, with Highlord Bolvar Fordragon as regent because King Varian Wrynn is missing. King Magni Bronzebeard rules Ironforge, High Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind leads the Night Elves, and High Tinker Mekkatorque leads the gnomes.

The **Horde** brings together Orcs, Trolls, Tauren and the Forsaken. Warchief Thrall leads it from Orgrimmar, Lady Sylvanas rules the Forsaken, High Chieftain Cairne Bloodhoof leads the Tauren, and Vol'jin leads the Darkspear trolls.

Neither side is simply good or evil. Each is a coalition with disagreements and compromises, and individual characters make hard choices. Forever keeps the factions separate, so where you travel and who helps you depends on the side you choose.

## Why the Skyborne are a new chapter

The **Skyborne** are Forever's headline new race. Their background links ancient Highborne ancestry with elemental wind spirits and a civilization tied to Skywall. Their starting land, **Zephras Isle**, is in crisis: the spirits that guarded them have vanished, structures are failing and communities are drifting apart.

Players choose between the Horde **Windshaper** and Alliance **High Order** traditions, and that choice also shapes class options. Windshapers can play Shaman, and High Order Skyborne can play Mage. Read the full [Skyborne guide](/guides/wow-forever-skyborne).

## Four new places and the questions they raise

### Zephras Isle: why did the protective spirits vanish?

The level 1–12 Skyborne start tells the story of a society whose old agreement with the wind spirits seems to be failing. Its mystery is about ancestry, duty and the future of an isolated people.

### Riverglades: who builds the frontier?

Blizzard describes the Riverglades as a new region of more than 150 quests, roughly for the mid-30s to mid-40s. Rivers, trade routes and old grudges make it a place where local politics can matter as much as world-ending threats.

### Shen'dralas: what remains of forgotten Highborne history?

A new adventure between Mulgore and Desolace ties into the Shen'dralar, the history of Eldre'Thalas (Dire Maul) and the region's centaurs. It asks about a past that has not fully disappeared.

### Mount Hyjal: what happens after a great victory?

Instead of replaying Archimonde's defeat, Forever asks what comes afterwards. How does a wounded land recover, and what dangers remain?

## Does WoW Forever follow the modern WoW story?

**No.** Forever returns to a post-Warcraft III foundation and develops its own continuity, so you should not import a character's later fate from modern WoW. When you read a lore guide, ask whether a claim is shared background, a Warcraft III event, something confirmed for Forever or something from a later timeline. A good article labels each.

## Learn lore while you play

You do not need days of timelines before your first character. Learn the story of where you stand: in Westfall, why farmers suffer and who the Defias are; in Silverpine, how the Forsaken came to be there; at a strange ruin, who built it.

That is what the free **[Lore Forever add-on](/downloads)** is for. It brings a large lore library into the game and narrated stories where recorded, with optional free voice and quest-dialogue packs. If you prefer to ask questions out loud, **[Willo](/willo)** is an optional Windows companion that works with it and is free to start. It does not turn a theory into canon, so quest text and Blizzard announcements still decide.

## Five lore questions for your first ten levels

1. **Who controls this region?** The answer explains the conflict around you.
2. **What happened here before I arrived?** Ruined farms and old roads have histories.
3. **Why is my faction involved?** The Alliance and Horde rarely act without reasons.
4. **Who are the named characters?** Even small quest givers can connect to larger events.
5. **What is unresolved?** An open mystery can point to the next zone or dungeon.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does WoW Forever take place?

WoW Forever takes place in the first year of World of Warcraft, a few years after the Third War and after the Forsaken Kingdom campaign, before Molten Core. It follows its own developing storyline.

### Do I need to play Warcraft III before WoW Forever?

No. Warcraft III gives helpful background, especially for Lordaeron and the Forsaken, but you can start Forever as a first visit to Azeroth.

### Is WoW Forever lore the same as modern WoW?

Some history, peoples and places are shared, but Forever's story is its own. Later events from modern WoW are not Forever canon.

### Who are the Forsaken in WoW Forever?

The Forsaken are undead people who broke free of the Scourge's control. They are ruled by Lady Sylvanas Windrunner from the Undercity and are working out survival and identity after Lordaeron's fall.

### Where do the Skyborne come from?

Blizzard describes them as descendants of Highborne exiles who made a pact with elemental wind spirits and built a civilization tied to Skywall. Their story begins on Zephras Isle.

### Can I follow WoW Forever lore without reading every quest?

Yes. Quest dialogue, zone introductions and tools such as the Lore Forever add-on give context. The add-on offers in-game entries and narration where recorded.
